Field notes

I practice what I teach.

Teaching crypto safety without living it would be noise. Here's how my own system runs, without the private numbers. Share the method, never the balances: that's one of the rules.

Research

Scored research with cited sources

Every asset I study gets a written research note with its sources. Scores are computed from a written formula and left blank until research backs them. Nothing is typed in from a hunch.

Market view

A market-direction model that admits when it's stale

I blend dated judgment pillars (policy, rates, geopolitics, tokenization) with a live price pillar, and I flag when the judgment is out of date instead of trusting the headline number.

Risk limits

Written risk rules that can halt trading

I test strategy in a paper-traded model portfolio with written limits, separation of duties (no one approves their own order) and an automatic halt. When a limit is breached, trading stops until the rules are re-tested. I keep to that, even in a falling market.

Records

A decision log and a risk register

Major decisions are written down before they're made, with the reasoning and the date. Each risk has a named control. That's the same Operating Manual the Citadel's final trial asks you to write.
